# Sustainable Character and Stage Ladder

Read this reference when extending chapters or deciding where a new character appears.

## Current review

The current 25-stage curve has:

- explicit small CP steps and one Boss wall in chapters 2–5;
- chapter-wide mechanics for energy disruption, control, and elite shielding;
- readable recommendation and fallback copy;
- extensive reuse of the same enemy and structure topology after chapter 1.

This proves deterministic progression reachability, not sufficient long-term encounter variety. Avoid extending the
campaign by appending only higher `recommended_power` and `enemy_power_bp`.

## Repeating seven-beat production unit

Use one character/content unit over roughly one chapter:

| Beat | Content job | Character job |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Foreshadow | show a new enemy behavior safely | no ownership required |
| 2. Weakness | let current roster solve it awkwardly | establish the opportunity |
| 3. Offer | grant, preview, trial, or signal the design | explain role and fallback |
| 4. Practice | isolate the new tactical verb | prove complete 1★ value |
| 5. Combine | mix the verb with a known demand | create formation choice |
| 6. Spotlight | make the role the cleanest optional answer | show contribution evidence |
| 7. Boss remix | combine all demands under timing pressure | preserve at least one fallback |

Do not always make Beat 3 a random draw. Critical-path access must remain deterministic or have a deterministic
existing-role route.

## Stage family budget

For each five-stage chapter, author at least:

- one new enemy action or target-selection rule;
- one new structure/hazard interaction;
- one spatial or timing variation;
- one recovery/reward beat;
- one Boss remix that changes player action rather than only HP.

Reuse visuals where necessary, but the runtime decision must differ.

## Roster exposure audit

After drafting a chapter:

1. count recommended and fallback appearances per owned/obtainable archetype;
2. flag roles with zero spotlight opportunities;
3. flag roles recommended before deterministic availability;
4. flag a role appearing in more than half of primary recommendations unless the chapter explicitly tests its faction;
5. simulate the intended role and at least one fallback at the same account budget;
6. compare battle events, completion state, and failure reason—not only CP and win/loss.

## Reward continuity

Link each chapter:

`new threat → useful blueprint/fragment opportunity → research/upgrade decision → immediate validation → Boss reward → next threat preview`

The reward should unlock a choice or anticipation within the next two stages. Avoid rewards that sit unused until a
later chapter or recommendations for roles the player cannot reasonably own.

## Player evidence

Ask uncoached players:

- What did this character let you do that you could not do cleanly before?
- When did you choose not to deploy it?
- What changed at 2★ or 3★?
- Which enemy or structure made you reconsider the formation?
- Could you name a viable fallback?

If players answer only “战力更高”, revise the character or encounter.
