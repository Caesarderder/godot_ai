# Player-learning loop

Use for goal comprehension, affordance discovery, tutorial learning, failure
attribution, recovery choice, mastery, and voluntary replay.

## Session contract

Define one observable player task and the target audience. Give participants no
solution or builder rationale. Do not recruit or contact participants without user
approval.

Author a stable playtest scenario and observation sheet before changing the game;
keep participant behavior natural even when scenario setup is controlled.

Observe:

- first action and time to meaningful control;
- hesitation, misclicks, route confusion, and ignored feedback;
- whether the player can state the goal, risk, choice, and result;
- how the player explains failure;
- whether recovery is discoverable;
- whether the player voluntarily retries or changes strategy.

Separate observation from interpretation. A questionnaire answer does not replace
behavioral evidence.

## Maker–critic loop

Turn one repeated player-visible failure into a bounded design hypothesis. Change
the real game, protect frozen gates, and test with a fresh participant or a
counterbalanced comparison when practical. Do not optimize only for one coached
participant.

Automated agents may exercise routes and expose obvious ambiguity, but cannot
certify target-player understanding or fun.

## Exit

Close required learning/feel claims only with the declared player evidence. If
participants are unavailable, prepare the session and mark the gate
`human_required`; do not silently downgrade it.
